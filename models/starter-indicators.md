# Starter Indicators for Organic Gardens

> Draft — to be refined collectively by participants.

## Principles

- Start small: 5–8 indicators maximum.
- Mix qualitative and quantitative.
- Focus on what is easy to observe and meaningful over time.
- Leave room for local adaptation.

## Proposed Starter Indicators

### 1. Soil Health

- **Observation**: Does the soil look alive? (worms, fungi, structure, smell)
- **Format**: Simple 1–5 rating + optional photo
- **Why it matters**: Soil health is a proxy for long-term garden resilience.

### 2. Biodiversity

- **Observation**: Variety of crops, flowers, insects, birds observed.
- **Format**: Count of crop types + count of insect/bird types per visit
- **Why it matters**: Diversity often correlates with stability and reduced pest pressure.

### 3. Yield Appropriateness

- **Observation**: Does the garden produce enough for the gardener's needs?
- **Format**: Qualitative rating (too little / enough / surplus) + optional weight/volume
- **Why it matters**: Yield matters, but relative to goals and context.

### 4. Workload Sustainability

- **Observation**: Does the garden require a sustainable amount of time and effort?
- **Format**: Hours per week + satisfaction rating
- **Why it matters**: A garden that burns out its gardener is not a high-quality garden.

### 5. Gardener's Joy

- **Observation**: How much pleasure does the garden bring?
- **Format**: 1–5 rating + one sentence
- **Why it matters**: Quality includes subjective well-being and motivation.

### 6. Resilience to Events

- **Observation**: How did the garden handle recent weather, pests, or other surprises?
- **Format**: Short narrative + 1–5 rating
- **Why it matters**: Resilience is a key dimension of qualitative evolution.

## How to Use

1. Pick the indicators that resonate with your garden.
2. Adapt the observation format to your habits.
3. Record at least once per month during the growing season.
4. Share and compare with the group to refine.
